# Output playbook: what a good answer looks like

The tool prints evidence. The answer is a decision. Follow this shape; adapt the wording to the user.

## 1. After `lj brief` (daily)

> **Three worth applying to today** (new since yesterday, all checked):
>
> 1. **ML Engineer Intern, Genentech** (South San Francisco). Fit 74: matches PyTorch, Python, NLP; missing Spark.
>    Posted 1 day ago, ~40 applicants, listed on Genentech's own Workday board (so real). Apply there, not through
>    LinkedIn: <direct link>. Pay not published.
> 2. ...
>
> **Heads-up:** one role (Acme, "Data Scientist") looks like a ghost job: open 94 days, not on Acme's own board. Skipping it.
> **Hiring surge:** Stripe posted 5 new roles this week; your profile fits two. Worth a note to a recruiter.
> **Follow-up due:** Notion (applied 7 days ago). Draft a 3-line note?
> **Learn next:** Docker appears in 41% of the postings you matched and is the only gap in 6 of them. A 2-day project
> that fixes it: containerise your existing API and publish the image size before/after.

## 2. After `lj resume` / `lj skills` / `lj require`

Group by decision, not by score:

- **Apply now** (fit high, fresh, verified or no red flags).
- **Stretch worth a shot** (name the exact gap and the cheapest way to close or work around it).
- **Skip** (one line each: needs a PhD, clearance, 5+ years, onsite in a country they excluded).

Always show: why it matched, the apply link, and the single next action. Offer CSV export.

## 3. After `lj verify`

State the label in plain words, then the evidence:

- *verified listing*: "Also on the employer's Greenhouse board, posted 3 days ago."
- *no red flags*: "Nothing suspicious, but I could not cross-check it against an employer board. Treat as unverified."
- *uncertain / likely ghost*: list the reasons that fired (age, missing from the board, evergreen wording, reposts).

Pay: quote it exactly with its source, or say "not published anywhere I can see". Never guess a number.

## 4. After `lj plan`

Lead with the verdict line, then: red flags, resume edits (true ones only), the ordered steps, and the people-search links.
If the user gave a resume, name the 2 to 3 matched skills to move to the top and the 1 to 2 gaps to address with a project.

## 5. Tone and honesty

- Direct and specific. No pep talk, no generic "tailor your resume" without naming what to change.
- Say "looks like" for anything heuristic. Say "unverified" when it is.
- If nothing good turned up, say so and widen the search (level mode `broad`, longer time window, more locations)
  before concluding there are no jobs.
